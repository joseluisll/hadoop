Does Jetty 12 break the ecosystem?

Yes — four of the six projects that could be tested, three of them building without a warning first. And yes, it can be fixed inside Hadoop.

Baseline trunk d57814e3 Candidate jetty-phase-c 91ccfd1f Covers HADOOP-19970 / 19971 / 19972

Verdict

Four confirmed downstream breaks, all reproduced by real builds: Tez fails to compile; Ozone, Hive and Knox compile cleanly and then cannot link Hadoop's HTTP stack.

The claim that this work is invisible to downstream projects is false. Whether that should block the change is a reviewer's decision — none of these are Hadoop API breaks, and two are arguably the downstream project's own fragility. But they are real, and a release note that does not mention them will surprise people.

Not universal, and the predicate is now known. Oozie pins Jetty 9 and comes through clean. What decides it is whether the project's own Jetty declaration wins Maven mediation for the core artifacts while Hadoop's ee8 modules arrive transitively — a property of declaration depth across the whole reactor, invisible from reading a POM.

Three of the four are fixable with no downstream action, and the fix has been built. Relocating Jetty into a published hadoop-shaded-jetty artifact — the pattern Hadoop already uses for Protobuf and Guava — removes the collision at the source. It compiles across all 119 modules, passes every invariant check, and produces zero test regressions. The fourth, Tez, needs two lines in Tez.

Why three projects compile and then fail

The compile step passes because the project's own Jetty 9 jars satisfy every import in its source. The failure is one level down, in what Hadoop drags along beside them.

Estimated downstream impact

Confirmed broken

  • Ozone, Hive, Knox — compile clean, fail at runtime. Every HTTP entry point that goes through Hadoop's HttpServer2 fails to start. Nothing in the build warns them.
  • Tez — fails at compile. Loud and immediate, so lower risk in practice.

Who else is likely affected

  • Any project that declares jetty-server 9.x directly and takes Hadoop transitively. That is the predicate, and it is checkable in one command: resolve the classpath and see whether jetty-server lands at 9 or 12 while jetty-ee8-* is present.
  • Any project borrowing a Jetty jar it never declares. Tez's shape. Rung 3 measured jetty-servlet leaving 34 module classpaths, jetty-webapp 30, the websocket family 29 each.

Not affected

  • Shaded-client consumers. Packaging invariants unchanged; Spark is the notable case.
  • Projects with no Jetty imports — HBase, Ranger.
  • Projects whose Jetty declaration loses mediation — Oozie, which gets a coherent Jetty 12 stack.

Can it be made non-breaking?

Yes, for the three link-time breaks — and it was built, not sketched. Below, in the order the question was actually worked: what a downstream project can do itself, why Hadoop cannot impose that, the shading approach that failed, the one that held, and what Tez still needs.

The downstream fix is one line

Tested directly against phase-c, with a consumer taking hadoop-common and hadoop-hdfs:

Consumer's POMjetty-server resolves toJetty 12 ee8
pins Jetty 9.4.589.4.58fails
no Jetty pin at all12.0.37links
pins Jetty 12.0.3712.0.37links

Drop the pin, or move it to 12. Either works.

Hadoop cannot force it

Maven resolves one version per artifact by nearest wins. A consumer's direct declaration is depth 1; Hadoop's transitive Jetty is depth 2 or more. No version, scope or ordering choice in Hadoop's POMs outranks it. That is a property of Maven, not of this change — and it rules out any fix that works by adjusting Hadoop's dependency declarations.

Shading inside each module does not scale

The obvious attempt — a maven-shade-plugin execution in hadoop-common relocating org.eclipse.jetty into org.apache.hadoop.shaded.… — builds clean in 47 seconds and, in isolation, works: 1,700 relocated classes, no leakage, and a consumer pinning Jetty 9 loads HttpServer2 again. Extended to the full reactor it failed three times in a row, each failure a variant of the same thing:

  • WebApps in YARN handing an unshaded WebAppContext to a shaded server.
  • The WebSocket packages absent from hadoop-common's shaded jar, so YARN could not find them at all.
  • WebServer needing a shaded WebAppContext accepted by an unshaded WebSocket API.

Relocation is all-or-nothing across every module that interoperates. Package names are the interface; shading one module renames half of a conversation. That rules out per-module shading, not shading itself.

The hadoop-thirdparty pattern — built, and it holds

One new module, hadoop-shaded-jetty, publishes Jetty 12 ee8 pre-relocated into org.apache.hadoop.thirdparty.org.eclipse.jetty; every consuming module imports from there. This is the same route Hadoop already took for Protobuf and Guava. org.eclipse.jetty.toolchain — the servlet API — is deliberately not relocated, because that is the contract downstream code compiles against.

The consumer keeps its Jetty 9, Hadoop uses its own relocated Jetty 12, and they no longer meet. That is Ozone, Hive and Knox fixed at the source, with no downstream change at all.

The one gate it initially failed is worth knowing about. BanDuplicateClasses fired because hadoop-client-runtime and -minicluster exclude Jetty by coordinate, and those excludes stop matching once Jetty arrives as one pre-relocated artifact. Two added <exclude> lines fix it — but any packaging rule naming Jetty by coordinate needs the same treatment.

Tez needs two lines, and they are in Tez

Shading cannot reach Tez, because Tez's problem is inheritance, not resolution: ProfileOutputServlet extends Jetty's DefaultServlet, borrowed from Hadoop's closure. Relocating that class moves it further away, not closer.

-import org.eclipse.jetty.servlet.DefaultServlet;
+import javax.servlet.http.HttpServlet;

-public class ProfileOutputServlet extends DefaultServlet {
+public class ProfileOutputServlet extends HttpServlet {

Verified safe by inspection: the class uses nothing from DefaultServlet — no super. call, no getResource, no ResourceService, no ContextHandler — and its doGet overrides completely. It never should have extended a Jetty class.

And ee8 is not the problem

Choosing the ee8 environment is already the minimal-disruption option: it keeps the javax.servlet package names, where ee9 or ee10 would move the servlet API to jakarta.servlet and break every consumer's imports outright. What breaks things here is Jetty 12 relocating its own core packages — Deployable, Handler.Singleton — which no environment choice avoids.

Claims that did not survive checking

Recorded so none of them resurfaces as received wisdom.

ClaimedActually
HttpServer2.webAppContext is a downstream-reachable break. Unreachable — final class, private constructor, so protected grants nobody access.
An Ozone-shaped POM proves the duplicate servlet API failure. The probe used 2 Hadoop artifacts and 2 exclusions; Ozone really uses 14 and 19, and shows no duplicate.
Any project pinning Jetty 9 is exposed. Oozie pins 9.3.27 and is fine. Mediation decides, not the pin.
Oozie could not be tested. A probe bug — hadoop-tools is a pom-only aggregator being declared as a jar.
HBase is the strongest detector to prioritise. Zero Jetty imports. It cannot detect this at all.
Shading hadoop-common alone is a workable route. Fails three ways at full-reactor scale. Relocation is all-or-nothing across modules that interoperate.
japi-compliance-checker 2.3 is the version to use. Deadlocks on larger jars — undrained stderr in its open3 call. 1.8 uses system() with redirects and does not. The 2.2× speedup was measured on one jar and generalised wrongly.

How to read this

Every number is a difference between two Hadoop refs built identically, minutes apart, into isolated local repositories. A check that fails the same way on both sides is reported as telling us nothing — Atlas and, before its probe was fixed, Oozie.

Nine projects is a sample, not proof, and three of them could not be tested at all. Of the six that could be, four break. The cheap checks were not merely incomplete — they were actively misleading, returning a clean result for every project that turned out to be broken.

The hadoop-thirdparty result is a working build in a branch, not a proposal: 74 files, every module compiling, every invariant rule green, and the failing-test set unchanged. What it is not is a full-Hadoop validation, and the sections above say so where it matters rather than in a caveat at the end.
